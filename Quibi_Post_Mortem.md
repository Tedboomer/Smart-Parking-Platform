# Technology Post-Mortem: Quibi

## Overview

Quibi was a short-form video streaming service created for mobile users. It launched in 2020 and focused on shows divided into short episodes that were generally less than ten minutes long.

The company raised approximately $1.75 billion and worked with major entertainment companies and well-known actors and producers.

However, Quibi struggled to attract and keep enough users and shut down in October 2020, only a few months after launching.

## What Went Wrong

One major problem was that Quibi entered a very competitive streaming market.

Users already had services such as Netflix, YouTube, Hulu, and Disney+, making it difficult for Quibi to convince people to pay for another streaming subscription.

Quibi also focused heavily on mobile-only viewing at launch. This limited how customers could watch the content.

Another problem was lower-than-expected user interest and viewership. Reports during the launch period showed that the company was already dealing with inactive users and lower viewer enthusiasm than expected. :chatgpt-content-reference{index="1"}

The COVID-19 pandemic also affected the service. Quibi was designed around the idea that users would watch short videos while traveling or commuting, but many people were staying home.

## Project Management Problems

The project involved a very large investment before the company proved that enough customers wanted the product.

The company also entered the market with a product strategy that was difficult to change quickly after launch.

More user testing and market validation before investing heavily in content may have helped identify some of these problems earlier.

## Lessons Learned

One lesson from Quibi is that having a large budget does not guarantee that a technology product will succeed.

Companies need to understand what customers actually want before investing heavily.

Products should also be flexible enough to adapt when customer behavior or market conditions change.

Testing the idea with smaller groups of users before a full launch can help reduce financial risk.

## Recommendations

For a similar future project, I would recommend starting with a smaller launch and collecting user feedback before spending heavily on content.

The product should support multiple viewing options instead of depending mainly on one device.

The company should also compare its product carefully against competitors and make sure customers have a strong reason to switch or pay for the new service.

Regular reviews of user activity and customer feedback should be used to make changes quickly after launch.
